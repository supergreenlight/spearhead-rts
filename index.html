02 The game

Build a company from the Wehrmacht or the Red Army, commit it to the front, and break the enemy headquarters — or out-hold them on victory points when the clock runs out.

SPEARHEAD strips away unit micromanagement. You never drag a selection box across a squad. What you decide is what to field, where to send it, and when — and that is the whole game. Matches run five to ten minutes, so a decision is rewarded or punished quickly.

  • Armor

    Penetration, not hit points

    Survival is armor, not a health bar. Every shot resolves against plate thickness, slope, impact angle and range. A round that gets through breaks a track, kills the engine, jams the turret or sets off the ammunition rack. A T-34 that reaches a Tiger's flank wins that exchange.

  • Command

    The commander's seat

    Direct infantry, vehicles and armor from above, and call in artillery and air support. Combat units advance on their own, so your attention goes to composition, timing and ground. Battles escalate through Contact, Deployment, Engagement and Decisive Engagement, and a doctrine tree unlocks heavier equipment as they do.

  • Ace

    Then fight it yourself

    When the battle tips, drop into your ace tank and fight through the gunsight — sights, ammunition, reload, all by hand. The ace fires under the same penetration rules as everything else, in the same match the commander is running.

Weapons as they were

Armor values, penetration, rate of fire and muzzle velocity come from historical specifications, not from a balance curve. German quality against Soviet quantity is the balance.

Infantry lives by cover

Squads use walls, trenches and buildings. Suppressed squads stay pinned, and killing a machine gunner only means the assistant takes the gun.
